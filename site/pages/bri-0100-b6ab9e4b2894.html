The historical Britannica passage on weighing machines describes a steelyard for weighing loaded carts, with mechanical details rendered nearly unintelligible by OCR errors, and traces the steelyard's history from Babylonians through Romans to Europe, including a claimed Pompeii find. The candidate Wikipedia passages on steelyard balance provide a clear modern account of the same instrument—unequal arms, sliding counterweight, law of the lever—and confirm its ancient use among Greeks and Romans, as well as the existence of cart steelyards for weighing waggons. The sources agree on the core identity and function of the steelyard and its use for heavy vehicles, but differ in language clarity, coverage of biblical philology, and specific artifact claims that cannot be verified from the provided excerpts.

Similarities

A steelyard is a straight-beam balance with unequal arms and a sliding counterweight on the longer arm.

Steelyards were used in antiquity, including among the Romans and at Pompeii.

Large steelyards or cart steelyards were used to weigh loaded carts and waggons.
