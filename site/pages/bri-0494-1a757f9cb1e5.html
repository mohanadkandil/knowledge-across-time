Summary

The Britannica sixth-edition passages concern Scots law of tailzies, entails, heirs of entail, and heirs of provision, with OCR corruption obscuring some wording. The Wikipedia Tailzie and Entail Act 1685 articles provide strong counterparts for the tailzie/entail material, confirming the concept, the 1685 codification, restrictions on alienation, and eventual abolition by the Abolition of Feudal Tenure etc. (Scotland) Act 2000. Key differences include the historical source's treatment of heirs of provision and marriage settlements, which have no counterpart in the supplied Wikipedia passages, and the shift from describing entail as operative law to describing its abolition.

Similarities

Tailzie is a Scots law concept governing the inheritance of immovable property according to a course laid out in a deed of tailzie.

The Entail Act 1685 codified tailzies.

An heir in tailzie could not sell the entailed property except to the feu superior.

When the heirs called in a tailzie come to succeed, the irritancies no longer operate and the fee becomes simple and unlimited.

Differences: language, knowledge and coverage

coverage

Historical source claim

Britannica discusses heirs of provision, marriage provisions, and their subordination to creditors' debts.

Modern source claim

Wikipedia does not discuss heirs of provision or marriage provisions in the supplied Tailzie or Entail Act 1685 articles.

The historical source covers additional inheritance categories (provisions to children by marriage contract, their priority relative to debts) that are absent from the candidate Wikipedia passages.

language

Historical source claim

Britannica uses OCR-noisy spellings such as "tailzie," "tail-ize," "irritancies," and "entailed."

Modern source claim

Wikipedia uses standardized spelling "tailzie" and explains the term derives from Old French tailler with a yogh represented by z.

The historical text's spelling is degraded by OCR, while the modern source provides a clean etymology and standardized terminology.

knowledge

Historical source claim

Britannica describes the legal mechanics of entails as operative law, including registration requirements and powers of heirs of entail.

Modern source claim

Wikipedia states that Part 5 of the Abolition of Feudal Tenure etc. (Scotland) Act 2000 disentailed all entailed land in Scotland and required closure of the Register of Entails.

The historical source presents entail law as in force, whereas the modern source reports its abolition in 2000 (effective 2004 per the Entail Act 1685 article).

Uncertainties

  • The Britannica text is heavily OCR-corrupted (e.g., "antherized," "promocatories," "pro- party"), so precise legal meanings in some passages cannot be reliably recovered from the supplied text alone.
  • The Britannica regions cite both 1485, c. 22 and the late George II for entail authorization, while Wikipedia attributes codification to the Entail Act 1685; the relationship between these statutes is not fully explained in the supplied texts.
  • The candidate Wikipedia corpus does not include articles on heirs of provision, marriage provisions, or rights of conjunct fees, so no modern counterpart exists for several Britannica passages.
  • The pinned Wikipedia corpus includes unrelated articles (Bhe v Magistrate, Khayelitsha; Earl of Selkirk) that do not clearly correspond to the Britannica subject matter.
