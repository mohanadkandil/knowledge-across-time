Summary

The historical Britannica UKRAINE entry and the modern Wikipedia Outline of Ukraine both identify Ukraine as a European country associated with Kyiv/Kiof and neighboring Russia and Poland. However, the historical entry describes Ukraine as territory contested and ultimately possessed by Russia and Poland, while the modern outline presents Ukraine as an independent country, formerly part of the Soviet Union, with detailed modern geography, demographics, and governance. The historical text is OCR-corrupted, limiting precise comparison.

knowledge

Historical source claim

By a treaty between Russia and Poland in 1603, Poland remained in possession of the part of Ukraine west of the Dnieper, while the east side, inhabited by Cossacks, was in better condition; by the 1793 partition treaty, the whole of Ukraine on both sides of the Dnieper belonged to Russia.

Modern source claim

Ukraine is a sovereign country, formerly part of the Soviet Union, with its own government, capital Kyiv, and international borders.

The historical entry treats Ukraine as territory divided between Russia and Poland and ultimately under Russian control, whereas the modern outline presents Ukraine as an independent state with its own political institutions.

coverage

Modern source claim

The modern source contains extensive geographic and demographic detail absent from the brief historical entry.
