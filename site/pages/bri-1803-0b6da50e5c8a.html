Summary

The historical Britannica text is too OCR-corrupted to support detailed comparison, but it briefly mentions aquatinta among engraving techniques that made improvements. The Wikipedia corpus provides a detailed modern account of aquatint as an intaglio tonal technique using powdered rosin and acid, developed around 1650–1780 and widely used from about 1770 to 1830, with named artists and works. A partial similarity exists in both sources treating aquatint as related to engraving/etching and noting its use in England, but the modern source offers far greater technical and historical detail.
