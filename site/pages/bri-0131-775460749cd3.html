Summary

The Britannica Third Edition entry and the Wikipedia article on Thomas Ruddiman describe the same Scottish classical scholar and grammarian, with strong agreement on his birth near Banff in October 1674, his father being a farmer, his schoolmastership at Laurencekirk, and his later work at the Advocates' Library in Edinburgh. Both also connect him to a Latin grammar publication. However, the Britannica text is heavily OCR-corrupted, making many names, dates, and details unreliable, and it includes additional anecdotes and specifics (university duration, an unpublished composition, teaching children, financial payments) not found in the Wikipedia article. These are coverage and language differences rather than clear factual contradictions, though the corruption prevents definitive verification of several historical claims.

Similarities

Thomas Ruddiman was a Scottish classical scholar and grammarian born in October 1674 near Banff/Banffshire to a farmer father.

Ruddiman served as schoolmaster at Laurencekirk before moving to Edinburgh to work at the Advocates' Library.

Ruddiman published 'Rudiments of the Latin Tongue', which was long used in Scottish schools.

Ruddiman edited the works of George Buchanan.
