The historical Britannica text describes Roman campaigns in Germany involving the Angrivarii, Germanicus, Arminius, and later conflicts with the Dacians under Decebalus and Trajan, but the text is heavily OCR-corrupted. The available Wikipedia passages confirm the Angrivarii's identity, location near the Weser, participation in Arminius's alliance, and later friendly relations with Rome and inclusion in Germanicus's triumph. No Wikipedia counterpart is available for the later Dacian war material, and the OCR corruption prevents a precise line-by-line comparison.

Similarities

The Angrivarii were a Germanic people living in northwest Germany near the Weser river and were mentioned by Roman authors Tacitus and Ptolemy.

The Angrivarii submitted to Roman protection after a formal defense.

Germanicus led Roman campaigns in Germany and recovered one of Varus's eagles.

After Arminius's death, the Angrivarii and other west Germans remained friendly toward Rome and were celebrated in Germanicus's triumph.

coverage

Historical source claim

Britannica provides a continuous narrative of Roman military operations in Germany from Germanicus through Claudius, Domitian, and into the Dacian wars under Trajan and Decebalus.

Modern source claim

The available Wikipedia excerpts focus narrowly on the Angrivarii people and their etymology, geography, and tribal relationships, with no coverage of the later Dacian campaigns.

The two sources cover different scopes: the historical Britannica text is a broad historical narrative, while the pinned Wikipedia corpus contains only targeted articles on the Angrivarii and a Germania disambiguation page.
