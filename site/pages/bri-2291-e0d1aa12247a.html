Summary
The Britannica eighth-edition chemistry excerpt and the Wikipedia Marsh test corpus both describe a sensitive arsenic detection method involving zinc, acid, arsine gas generation, ignition, and deposition of metallic arsenic on a cold surface. They agree on the core procedure and the test's sensitivity, and both note antimony as a potential interferent. However, the Britannica text is heavily OCR-corrupted, contains outdated or incorrect chemical formulas (e.g., AsO2 as an arsenic chloride), and includes topics (tellurium, poisoning treatment with iron/magnesia) absent from the provided Wikipedia corpus. Wikipedia adds historical context (John Bodle case, 1836 publication, 1840 Lafarge case) and modern distinctions (sodium hypochlorite test to distinguish arsenic from antimony/bismuth) not found in Britannica.

strong
The Wikipedia Marsh test article describes the same arsenic detection method found in the Britannica regions: generating arsine gas by adding zinc and acid to a suspected fluid, igniting the gas, and depositing metallic arsenic on a cold surface held in the flame. It also notes the historical origin of the test in the 1832 John Bodle case and James Marsh's development of the apparatus, which aligns with Britannica's description of the method's sensitivity and use.
