Summary

The historical Britannica entry for Dr Roebuck and the Wikipedia article on John Roebuck describe the same English industrialist and chemist, with strong correspondences in his work on sulphuric acid, iron manufacturing, and the Carron Company. However, the Britannica text is severely corrupted by OCR errors, yielding implausible dates (e.g., death in 1759 vs. Wikipedia's 1794) and garbled names. The Wikipedia account provides clearer, internally consistent details about Roebuck's life, partnerships, financial difficulties, and death. The Roemer passages in the Britannica text appear to concern a different individual (Ole Rømer) and are also heavily corrupted.

Differences: language, knowledge and coverage

knowledge

Historical source claim

Dr Roebuck died on 17 July 1759.

Modern source claim

John Roebuck died in Edinburgh in 1794.

The Britannica text appears to contain an OCR or transcription error, rendering the death year as 1759, while the Wikipedia article states 1794. The Britannica text also says the first furnace was blown in January 1790, which is chronologically impossible if he died in 1759, suggesting the historical text itself is corrupted.

knowledge

Historical source claim

Dr Roebuck established a manufacturer of useful articles in Scotland in 1729.

Modern source claim

John Roebuck introduced leaden condensing chambers for sulphuric acid in 1746 and built a factory at Prestonpans in 1749.

The dates and specific achievements differ substantially, likely due to OCR corruption in the Britannica text (1729 vs 1746/1749).

coverage

Historical source claim

Britannica describes Dr Roebuck's financial ruin, creditors, and his widow left without provision.

Modern source claim

Wikipedia describes Roebuck's financial difficulties, sale of his share in Watt's engine to Matthew Boulton, and continued management of the Bo'ness works.

Both sources cover Roebuck's financial decline, but with different details and emphasis. The Britannica text emphasizes his creditors and widow's lack of support, while Wikipedia specifies the Boulton debt cancellation and pottery venture.

language

Historical source claim

Britannica uses archaic spellings and OCR-corrupted text (e.g., 'Roeback', 'wefeels', 'glafs', 'fullestate sup- tal').

Modern source claim

Wikipedia uses modern standardized English spelling and grammar.

The historical text exhibits long 's' forms and severe OCR errors, making some passages difficult to read, whereas the Wikipedia text is in contemporary English.

Uncertainties

  • The Britannica text is heavily corrupted by OCR errors, making exact dates, names, and claims unreliable (e.g., death year 1759 vs 1794, 'Chillam V King of Damals' appears to be a corruption of Christian V of Denmark).
  • The Britannica entry for Roemer (regions 10-13) appears to be about Ole Rømer, the Danish astronomer, not John Roebuck, and is heavily OCR-corrupted; it is unclear whether this was intended as part of the same entry or a separate entry.
  • The exact year of the sulphuric acid manufacture establishment in the Britannica text (1729) conflicts with Wikipedia (1746), but this may be an OCR error.
  • It is unclear whether the Britannica 'Dr Roebuck' and Wikipedia 'John Roebuck' are the same person given the corrupted dates, though the occupational and biographical details strongly suggest they are.
