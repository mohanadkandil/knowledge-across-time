Summary

The historical Britannica supplement and the Wikipedia passages both describe Napoleon Bonaparte's 1796 Italian campaign, including the battles around Millesimo and Dego, the defeat of Beaulieu and Provera, and the Armistice of Cherasco with Sardinia. However, the Britannica text is heavily OCR-corrupted, rendering many names, numbers, and details unreliable. The Wikipedia corpus provides clearer, modern accounts of the same events but does not cover the full campaign narrative found in the Britannica regions (e.g., the Po River crossing and Lodi). Differences are primarily due to language degradation and coverage scope rather than direct factual contradiction, though some details cannot be reconciled due to uncertainty in the historical text.

Similarities

Bonaparte attacked and defeated Beaulieu's Austrian forces near Dego/Millesimo in April 1796, separating the Austrian and Piedmontese armies.

Provera retreated to Cosseria Castle and surrendered after a siege, allowing the French advance to continue.

An armistice was signed with the King of Sardinia at Cherasco, followed by a formal peace treaty, imposing harsh conditions including fortress surrenders and territorial cessions.

Differences: language, knowledge and coverage

language

Historical source claim

The Britannica text uses archaic/OCR-corrupted spellings such as "Drgo," "Millefimo," "Benulieu," "Amrilice," and "figigned."

Modern source claim

The Wikipedia text uses modern standardized spellings such as "Dego," "Millesimo," "Beaulieu," "armistice," and "signed."

The historical text appears to be heavily OCR-degraded, making direct lexical comparison difficult, while the Wikipedia text is in modern English.

knowledge

Historical source claim

The Britannica text states that after the armistice, Bonaparte was allowed to "conti the Pon t Murderia" (likely "continue the Pont Murelia" or similar) and that the French could cross Piedmont.

Modern source claim

The Wikipedia text states the armistice withdrew Sardinia from the War of the First Coalition and handed over Alessandria, Coni, and Tortone to France, with free passage for French troops.

Both describe the same armistice, but the Britannica text is too corrupted to verify exact terms, while the Wikipedia text provides clear, specific terms.

coverage

Historical source claim

The Britannica text includes a detailed narrative of the campaign from Millesimo through the Armistice of Cherasco and the advance toward the Po, including the crossing at Piacenza and battles at Pombio and Lodi.

Modern source claim

The provided Wikipedia passages cover Millesimo, Dego, and the Armistice of Cherasco, but do not include the later Po River crossing, Pombio, or Lodi details found in the Britannica text.

The historical source has broader campaign coverage in the supplied regions; the Wikipedia corpus is limited to specific battles and the armistice.

uncertain

Historical source claim

The Britannica text describes a battle at Dego where French General Gaulfs was killed and the Austrians retired with "14 mo code preferences" (unclear).

Modern source claim

The Wikipedia text on the First Battle of Dego describes a 1794 battle with no mention of General Gaulfs; the Wikipedia text on the Second Battle of Dego (implied in Millesimo articles) describes the April 1796 engagement.

The OCR corruption in the Britannica text makes it impossible to determine whether it refers to the First or Second Battle of Dego, or whether the casualty details match modern accounts.

Uncertainties

  • The Britannica text is severely OCR-corrupted, making many proper names, numbers, and specific claims unreliable (e.g., "Drgo," "Millefimo," "Benulieu," "Gaulfs," "14 mo code preferences").
  • It is unclear whether the Britannica Dego passage refers to the 1794 First Battle of Dego or the 1796 Second Battle of Dego.
  • The exact date and terms of the armistice in the Britannica text cannot be verified due to corruption (e.g., "amrilice was figigned on the 20th" vs. Wikipedia's 28 April).
  • The Britannica text's reference to "Paris)" in region-6 appears to be an OCR error for a location in Italy, but the intended word is unrecoverable from the supplied text.
