Though the expense of lighting by gas from oil appears, on a large scale, not to be much less than double that of coal gas, the same great dispropier will not altogether hold for very limited quantities of light where the charge for an unrelated forms a considerable part of the current expenditure. A cool gas apparatus, however small, will, when in work, require the name, constant attention of a person to manage the fire, to charge and discharge the retorter, to remove the lime or other substance used for purification, and to remove that while his done ie office, also to empty the tar reservoir, &c. In the other apparatus, when the oil reservoir is filled and once set to work, it requires an other attendance for a number of hours, then the keeping of the fire in order, and the production of gas can be contained at pleasure, stopped by the mere turning of a cock, and the manances accompanying the formation and removal of tur, ammonical liquor, and lime relics, are entirely modeled.

Summary

The Britannica supplement details the costs and by-products of coal gas and oil gas lighting, including a specific description of an Argand oil lamp consuming 60 grains of oil per hour to produce light equal to 3 candles, and comparative cost tables ranking tallow, oil, white oil gas, and coal gas. The Wikipedia corpus includes articles on the Argand lamp, confirming its brightness and eventual replacement by kerosene lamps around 1850, and lists of gas companies/boards without comparable cost data. The sources agree on the Argand lamp's performance but differ in coverage: Britannica provides 19th-century engineering economics, while Wikipedia covers later technological transitions and organizational history. coverage

Historical source claim

Britannia provides detailed annual expenditure figures for coal gas and oil gas apparatus, including retorts, gasmeters, main pipes, management, wages, and renewal costs.

Modern source claim

The Wikipedia corpus does not provide comparable annual expenditure figures for gas lighting apparatus; it lists gas companies and boards without cost breakdowns.

The historical source focuses on engineering economics of gas production, while the modern sources focus on corporate and organizational history of gas boards.

knowledge

Historical source claim

Britannia states that coal gas costs about 22d for a light equal to 1 lb. of tallow, and oil gas about 4jd, with coal gas to oil gas cost ratio nearly as ten to six.

Modern source claim

Wikipedia states that kerosene lamps replaced Argand/oil lamps around 1850 because kerosene was cheaper than vegetable oil and produced a whiter flame.

The historical source compares tallow, oil, white oil gas, and coal gas lighting costs in the early 19th century; the modern source describes a later technological transition to kerosene, which is not discussed in the Britannica excerpt.

Modern source claim

Wikipedia uses modern standardized terminology such as "Argand lamp," "candelas," "kerosene," and "vegetable oil."

The historical text reflects early 19th-century spelling and OCR noise, while the modern source uses contemporary English.
